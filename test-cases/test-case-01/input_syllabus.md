# Our Sustainable School: Small Actions, Big Difference

**Document type:** Teacher-created unit syllabus — synthetic test data  
**Subject:** European Hours  
**Level:** Primary P4-P5  
**Duration:** 6 weeks, 90 minutes per week  
**Working language:** English; adaptable for multilingual sections

## 1. Rationale

Pupils encounter messages about climate change, waste and responsible behaviour in their daily lives. This unit introduces practical environmental actions that can be taken at school and highlights examples from different European countries. It aims to raise awareness and encourage pupils to make greener choices.

## 2. General objectives

- Understand why protecting the environment is important.
- Learn simple ways to recycle and save energy and water.
- Work cooperatively in a small group.
- Communicate environmental messages through a poster and a short presentation.

## 3. Learning outcomes

By the end of the unit, pupils will be able to:

- identify common recyclable and non-recyclable materials;
- describe at least three ways to save energy or water at school;
- name examples of environmental actions used by European schools or cities;
- search selected websites and record relevant facts;
- work in a group to produce a clear poster;
- present one action that the class could take to help the environment.

## 4. Core content

- Waste, litter and basic recycling rules.
- Everyday energy and water use.
- Selected examples of “green schools” and city initiatives in Europe.
- Responsible behaviour at school and at home.
- Key vocabulary: sustainability, recycling, renewable energy, waste, pollution.

## 5. Pedagogical approach

Teaching will combine short teacher presentations, videos, worksheets, guided internet research, group discussion and poster making. The teacher will select the environmental problem, the research sources, the group roles and the actions to be considered. Pupils will follow the task instructions and share their findings.

## 6. Six-week learning sequence

| Week | Focus | Learning activities | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is sustainability? | Teacher slideshow, key vocabulary and a short whole-class quiz. | Completed vocabulary sheet |
| 2 | Waste and recycling | Sort pictures of materials into categories; review local recycling rules. | Recycling worksheet |
| 3 | Energy and water | Use a teacher-provided checklist to observe lights, taps and classroom equipment. | Classroom checklist |
| 4 | European examples | In groups, search selected websites for one school or city initiative and record facts. | Research notes |
| 5 | Our greener school | Create a group poster entitled “Ten Things Our School Should Do” using the supplied template. | Group poster |
| 6 | Share and act | Give a three-minute presentation, complete the quiz and join a supervised playground clean-up. | Presentation and clean-up |

## 7. Assessment

| Component | Weight | Main criteria |
|---|---:|---|
| Participation | 20% | Attention, task completion and cooperation |
| Knowledge quiz | 30% | Correct use of vocabulary and factual accuracy |
| Poster and presentation | 50% | Accurate information, clear presentation, neatness and shared speaking |

## 8. Resources

Teacher-selected websites, short videos and slides; recycling picture cards; worksheets; observation checklist; tablets or laptops; poster materials; gloves and bags for the clean-up.

## 9. Inclusion and multilingual support

Key vocabulary will be introduced with images and may be translated into pupils’ language sections. Mixed-ability groups, sentence starters and teacher-selected roles will support participation. Pupils may present with notes or pre-record part of their contribution where necessary.

## 10. Expected final product

Each group will produce a poster and a three-minute presentation proposing ten actions for a greener school. The posters will be displayed in the corridor. The class will also take part in one playground clean-up session.

## 11. Local adaptation

Teachers may replace websites or examples and may adjust the vocabulary, timing and group size to suit the school context. The final poster format and assessment weights should remain the same across groups.

## 12. European dimension

The unit should include examples from at least three European countries. Where possible, pupils should compare school routines and learn key environmental vocabulary used by classmates in other language sections. A partner class may be invited to view the final posters.

## 13. Teacher preparation checklist

- Select age-appropriate websites, videos and two European case examples.
- Prepare the vocabulary sheet, observation checklist and final quiz.
- Assign pupils to mixed-ability groups and allocate roles.
- Confirm supervision and waste-disposal arrangements for the clean-up.

## 14. Review after the unit

After the final presentation, the teacher will record which activities engaged pupils, whether the planned outputs were completed and which resources should be changed before the unit is taught again.
